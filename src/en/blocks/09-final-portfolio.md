## The idea of the block

The end of the year should give you something more concrete than a “show in a session”: **a portfolio page** you can show outside the club — to parents, a college or university admissions committee, a future employer. One of the year’s projects (most often the flagship object from [[block:5|block 5]], already animated and shown in AR) is brought to a presentable state and laid out on your own page of the club’s site ([[term:github-pages|GitHub Pages]]): a GLB model through [[term:model-viewer|model-viewer]], screenshots of the intermediate checkpoints, a short description of the path from silhouette to finished result.

::: idea Choose the project that represents you best
It needn’t be the flagship. If you are prouder of another object, that is an acceptable choice too. What matters is **one finished, well-documented project**, not a cursory overview of the whole year.
:::

This block is deliberately open: the number of sessions is flexible (roughly 2–3), and the pace and content depend on where you have got to by the end of the year. There are no new tools.

## Session 21 — choosing a project and final polish

First say which project of the year will be the basis of the page and one thing you want to improve in it. Then an open workshop: refine the chosen project with the whole toolkit of the year (detailing, color, topology, rig). At the end, note down: do you already have a [[term:glb|GLB]] export ([[block:7|block 7]]) and an AR page ([[block:8|block 8]])? If so, it’s a ready foundation and only the layout remains; if not, that is the priority for the next session.

## Session 22 — the portfolio page

The structure of a good page:

1. **the project’s title**;
2. **a GLB model** through `<model-viewer>` (the same as in block 8);
3. **3–4 screenshots of the journey**: silhouette → details → color → final (this is where `_step1.blend`, `_step2.blend`… come in handy);
4. **a short description**: what the project is, the hardest part, what you learned.

Take the [[ex:ar-showcase|AR showcase template]] as your base: drop in the model, add screenshots and text.

::: checkpoint
A published portfolio page with a GLB model, screenshots of the journey and a short description: something you can show outside the club. Swap links and look at each other’s pages.
:::

## Session 23 — the public showcase / demo day

The end of the year is a showcase of projects wider than a single session: parents, other clubs, the college administration. Everyone presents their portfolio page and, if they wish, shows the AR version of the model live on a phone. If there is a final 3D-printed model, it stands next to the screen as a third way to see the same work.

::: challenge
Prepare and rehearse a live talk of 1–2 minutes: not reading the description from the page but telling about the idea and the journey. Write down and say aloud 3–4 sentences in advance.
:::

## Common problems

| Problem | Why it happened | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The portfolio page looks empty or unconvincing | Only the final result is shown, without the path to it | Add 3–4 screenshots from the intermediate `_step*.blend` files: the path is what the reader finds most interesting |
| The project description sounds abstract (“made a cool model”) | It was written at the last moment, with no specifics | Answer three questions in writing: what it is, the hardest part, what you learned — and build the description from those answers |
| The demo-day talk falls apart: you forget what to say | There is no prepared and rehearsed text | Write and say aloud 3–4 sentences beforehand; don’t improvise for the first time on demo day itself |

## Before the session

- Public hosting for your page (GitHub Pages) works **long before session 22**, not at the last moment.
- The intermediate versions of your files (`_step*.blend`) and screenshots.
- A week before demo day, prepare a short spoken talk.
