## What it trains

The skill the club works on all year: **seeing simple shapes inside a complex object** and telling a true rotation of a figure from its mirror image. The first is the basis of a [[term:blockout|blockout]] ([[block:1]]); the second is understanding how [[term:mirror|Mirror]] works.

## Two modes

1. **“Break it into shapes.”** In front of you is a three-dimensional object (a mug, a lamp, a rocket, a robot, a mushroom, a chess pawn…). Rotate it with the mouse or your finger and mark every **type** of [[term:primitive|primitive]] you see: box, sphere, cylinder, cone, torus, plane. The number of repeats doesn’t matter — only the types.
2. **“Update the rotation.”** On the left is the target, on the right three candidates. One is the same figure, just rotated; another is its **mirror image**; the third is a completely different figure. Rotate both the target and the candidates: a true rotation matches the target cube for cube, while in a mirror image the colored cubes land in the opposite order however you turn it.

## Tips

- In mode 1, find the biggest shape first, then whatever is “attached” to it. That is exactly what you do in a blockout.
- In mode 2, don’t compare figures “by eye” in one position: rotate them until one cube matches the target, then check the rest.
- A mirror image can’t be obtained by rotation — which is why [[term:mirror|Mirror]] in Blender gives you a new half, not a “rotated” old one.
