The year’s program

Ten blocks from finding your way around the interface to a portfolio page. Every block ends with a checkpoint and has a challenge.

#The block map

00The Blender interfaceThe viewport, axis navigation, selection, the object hierarchy and property panels — before your first blockout. 1 session01Your first object in one sessionFive tools, mirror symmetry and face colors — no UV unwrapping. 2 sessions02Extended toolkit and detailingKnife, Subdivision Surface, Boolean and Merge: asymmetric forms and assembly from parts. 2–3 sessions03Modeling from referencesDon’t invent proportions — check them against an image right in the viewport. 3–4 sessions04Topology and retopologyWhy “just making the shape” is not enough: will this mesh survive movement? 2–3 sessions05Your own game object — the flagship projectThe biggest project of the half-year, from your own idea: silhouette, details, palette, pose. 4–5 sessions06Rigging in 15 minutes (and a bit more)One repeatable Rigify sequence to bring your own object to life with a simple loop. 2–3 sessions07Preparing a model for a game engineThree things that break an export: transforms, scale and normals. 2–3 sessions08An AR showcase without special hardwareYour own model in augmented reality on your own phone — via a link or a QR code. 2 sessions09The final project and your portfolio pageOne finished, well-documented project — and a page you can proudly show outside the club. 2–3 sessions
Note

Session numbers are approximate: the club’s pace depends on the group, so blocks may shift. The order of blocks is fixed: each one builds on the previous.

#Checkpoints and challenges

Every block ends with a real artifact (the checkpoint) and offers a harder version for those who move faster (the challenge).

#Guiding rules

  1. Every block ends with a real artifact you can show — not an exercise for its own sake.
  2. Intermediate versions are saved as separate files (_step1.blend, _step2.blend…): miss a session and you continue from a checkpoint.
  3. Every task has a challenge — not a new track, but a harder version of the same task.
  4. Show-and-tell every 2–3 sessions: everybody briefly shows their screen, the others say what worked and add one piece of advice.
  5. Self-expression comes first. You choose the object in every block (within the block’s requirements); the difficulty adapts, the topic doesn’t.
  6. Break down → work on the parts → assemble at the end. A complex idea isn’t built as one solid piece: you split it into logical parts, make each a separate object and assemble them at the end. This strategy appears in Block 2 and becomes key in Block 5.

#What the program builds on

The program is based on proven methods rather than invented from scratch: a five-hotkey blockout and color without UV unwrapping (Imphenzia), one project through the full cycle with checkpoint files (Blender Guru), a quick character build-out and references (Grant Abbitt), clean topology through simple shapes (CG Cookie), an up-to-date 2026 checklist from Blender to a game engine, and <model-viewer> for AR from a phone. The full list is on the Resources page.
